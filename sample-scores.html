Sample scores

Fifteen pieces of sheet music to import into Scorly, so that you have something to read, mark up and put into a setlist without hunting for your own files first.

Download

scorly-sample-scores.zip — 2.4 MB, twelve PDFs and three JPEG images of a page. Unzip it on the device you are testing on, then use Import in Scorly and pick as many files as you like at once.

Nothing here is precious. Import all of it, delete what you do not want, and do not worry about spoiling anything.

Two ways in, and both are worth trying

The twelve PDFs and the three images go in through the same Import button, but they are different paths through the app. The images are there so the photograph route — the one you would use on a page out of your own shelf — gets tried as well.

What is in it

A few were chosen for a reason. Good King Wenceslas, Greensleeves, Cavallini and Abt are a single page, which should open and should not offer to turn. Beethoven's Symphony No. 7 is 55 pages, which is the one to open if you want to know whether page turns stay quick in something long. Bach's Toccata and Fugue is eleven pages of dense organ writing — the hardest thing here to read at a stand.

Where it comes from, and why you may keep it

Every file is from the Mutopia Project, and every one of them is Public Domain — the music because of its age, and the engraving because the person who typeset it placed it in the public domain. Each page says so in its own footer, so you can check it without taking our word for it.

That mattered in the choosing. A modern edition of an old piece can carry rights of its own in the typesetting, so a page out of your own shelf is not automatically free to pass around. These are. The three JPEGs were made from three more Mutopia PDFs by rendering the page at 1555×2200; they are otherwise the same material.
