Scorly

Sheet music on a tablet, for people who play from it.

Scorly holds your scores and setlists on your own device, turns pages with a tap or a pedal, and lets you write on the page the way you would on paper.

What it does

  • Your library, on your device. Import PDFs and photographs of music, scan pages with the camera, or share a score into Scorly from another app. Scorly copies each file into its own storage, so nothing breaks when you tidy up your Downloads folder.
  • Tags. Group pieces your own way — a wedding list, a Tuesday trio — and filter the library or your setlists with one tap.
  • Reading, not browsing. Full screen, screen kept awake, page turns by tap, by keyboard, or by a Bluetooth page-turner pedal. Two pages side by side on a wide screen, when you switch it on.
  • Annotations. Pen, highlighter and eraser, written on the page and kept with it. The file you imported is never rewritten.
  • Sticky notes. Pin a note to the spot on the page it is about, write or type on it, and close it to a small mark.
  • Blank staves. Start a score on empty staff paper and write the music in yourself.
  • Setlists. Put pieces in order, play straight through them, lock a set so it cannot be changed by accident on stage. Each set has its own stage paper for the running order or the programme you were handed.
  • Backup you can hold. Export your whole library as one file, or have Scorly back it up to your Google Drive on a schedule you choose.

No account, and no server of ours

Scorly has no sign-up, no profile, and nowhere for your music to go that you did not send it. Backups go to a cloud account that is already yours, into a folder you can see. Diagnostics are off until you turn them on. The privacy policy says exactly what leaves the device and when.

Where it is

Scorly for Android is in closed testing on Google Play, and not yet available to everyone. An iOS version is planned. There is no mailing list; this page is where the news will be.

Support

Questions, faults, and feature requests go to support@scorlyapp.com. It is read by the person who writes the app.
